#### Detailed description

The Importance of Olfaction Questionnaire captures how much weight individuals place on their sense of smell in everyday life, rather than how well they can actually smell. The original instrument was developed by Croy and colleagues with three subscales (association, application, consequence); the version adopted here uses a fourth, aggravation, that was later developed. Responses are made on a four-point Likert scale scored from 0 (complete disagreement) to 3 (complete agreement). Three subscales draw on six items each (association, application, consequence) and the aggravation subscale on two items, giving twenty items in total. The subscales capture, respectively: the emotions, memories and episodes that smells evoke (association); the everyday use of smell (application); the readiness to act on what is smelled (consequence); and the degree to which smells are experienced as bothersome (aggravation).

The two-item aggravation subscale was originally added by Croy and colleagues as a control intended to detect respondents with a tendency to overstate the importance of olfaction; as such, its intent was to flag exaggerated or inflated responses.

Because the questionnaire was administered both digitally and on paper, with the same participants completing both, the equivalence of the two administration modes could be examined. In the validation underlying this work the digital version was completed first and the paper version second, with a variable interval in between, so the two administrations also serve as a test-retest measure.

**Digital version.** Participants complete the twenty items through an online form, responding to each statement on the 0 to 3 agreement scale. The labels identifying which subscale each item belongs to are not shown to participants. Subscale totals are obtained by summing the relevant items, and a composite total is formed across all items.

**Analog version.** Participants complete the identical twenty items on a paper form using the same 0 to 3 response scale at a later session. The labels identifying which subscale each item belongs to are not shown to participants. Subscale totals are obtained by summing the relevant items, and a composite total is formed across all items.

#### Number of participants (raw)

Each dataset holds one row per administration, keyed by SONA ID.

| Version | Raw rows | Unique IDs | Complete totals |
|---------|----------|------------|-----------------|
| Digital | 377      | 353        | 353             |
| Analog  | 208      | 207        | 183             |

196 participants started both the digital and the analog version, but only 183 fully completed both.

#### Conditions and number of trials

* Twenty items, each rated on a four-point scale from 0 (complete disagreement) to 3 (complete agreement).
* Two administrations: digital (online form) and analog (paper form), identical in content.
* Subscales: association (6 items), application (6 items), consequence (6 items) and aggravation (2 items).

#### Behavioural Measures

* Twenty item-level responses (0 to 3) per administration
* Association subscale total
* Application subscale total
* Consequence subscale total
* Aggravation subscale total
* Composite IOQ total
