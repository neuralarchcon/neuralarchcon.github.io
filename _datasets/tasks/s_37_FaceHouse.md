#### Detailed description

This task comprises two variants of a face/house discrimination paradigm. Both use a three-alternative forced-choice (3-AFC) judgment followed by a 5-point confidence rating, and share the same trial structure: blank screen (600 ms), fixation cross (400 ms), stimulus presentation (300 ms), followed by a response window. Face and house images were drawn from standardized stimulus sets (320 images per category); all images were grayscale and 10 × 10 degrees of visual angle. Stimulus parameters were presented using the method of constant stimuli, with all predefined levels appearing in randomized order across trials. Responses were made using arrow keys (left = face, right = house, down = third category), and confidence was rated on a 5-point scale (1 = low, 5 = high) using keys Q, W, E, R, and SPACE.

**Noise variant (Face/House/Noise).** Participants categorized noisy faces, noisy houses, and pure noise stimuli. The task was designed to measure perceptual categorization ability under varying signal-to-noise ratios and to induce illusory perceptions through added noise. Specifically, by interleaving pure noise stimuli (containing no structured image content) with noisy face and noisy house images, the task created conditions under which participants occasionally perceived faces or houses within pure noise stimuli, often with relatively high confidence. This allowed quantification of false perception rates as a measure of illusory perception. Stimuli were generated by combining a base image (face or house) with additive white noise. The noise level was held constant at 130 (out of 255 RGB values), while the signal level varied between 0 and 30 in steps of 2, resulting in 16 distinct levels. On pure noise trials (signal level = 0), stimuli consisted entirely of random pixel values with no structured image content.

**Blank variant (Face/House/Blank).** Participants categorized grayscale faces, grayscale houses, and blank (uniform gray) stimuli. The task was designed to measure perceptual categorization ability under conditions of varying signal intensity (luminance). Stimuli were generated by scaling the luminance of each base image (face or house); no noise was added. The signal level (luminance intensity) varied between 0 and 10 in steps of 1, resulting in 11 distinct levels. On blank trials, stimuli consisted of a uniform gray field matched in mean luminance to the face and house stimuli at each signal level, containing no structured image content.

#### Conditions and number of trials

**Noise variant:**

* Noise level: held constant at 130 (out of 255 RGB values)
* Signal level: varied between 0 and 30 in steps of 2, resulting in 16 distinct levels
* Practice trials: 10 trials
* Formal trials: 320 trials (divided into 2 blocks)

**Blank variant:**

* Noise level: held constant at 0
* Signal level: varied between 0 and 10 in steps of 1, resulting in 11 distinct levels
* Practice trials: 10 trials
* Formal trials: 110 trials (1 block)

#### Behavioural Measures

Both variants record the same two measures:

* Perceptual discrimination (3-AFC): participants indicated whether the stimulus contained a face, a house, or the third category (pure noise in the noise variant, a blank in the blank variant), using arrow keys (left = face, right = house, down = noise/blank).
* Confidence rating (5-point scale): following each perceptual judgment, participants rated their confidence using keys Q (1 = low confidence), W (2), E (3), R (4), and SPACE (5 = high confidence).
