#### Detailed description

Three tasks were administered using a contact thermode applied to the volar forearm.

**Task 1: Standard QST.** Participants underwent a thermal QST protocol adapted from the German Research Network on Neuropathic Pain (DFNS) protocol. Following a brief practice round on the dorsum of the hand, the thermode was placed on the volar forearm at a baseline temperature of 32 °C. Five measures were obtained: Cold Detection Threshold (CDT), Warm Detection Threshold (WDT), Thermal Sensory Limen (TSL), Cold Pain Threshold (CPT), and Heat Pain Threshold (HPT). For detection thresholds, participants pressed a stop button upon first perceiving a temperature change. For pain thresholds, they pressed the stop button when the thermal sensation acquired an additional painful quality (e.g., burning, stinging, drilling, or aching).

**Task 2: Paradoxical Heat Sensation (PHS) paradigm.** This task followed the procedure described by Mitchell et al. (2024, Communications Psychology). The thermode was placed on the opposite forearm compared to Task 1. The task comprised two subtasks (TSL Detection and TSL Pain), each performed at three baseline temperatures (32 °C, 38 °C, and 44 °C). In both subtasks, participants waited for an auditory cue before responding. In TSL Detection trials, participants pressed the stop button upon detecting any temperature change after the cue and reported the perceived quality (warm, cold, hot, painfully hot, or painfully cold). In TSL Pain trials, participants pressed the stop button when they perceived a painful sensation after the cue. The use of elevated baseline temperatures (38 °C and 44 °C) was designed to increase the likelihood of eliciting paradoxical heat sensations during cooling phases.

**Task 3: Temporal Summation of Pain.** A CHEPS (Contact Heat Evoked Potential Stimulator) thermode was placed on the forearm. A single heat pulse was first delivered and participants rated its painfulness on a 0–100 numerical rating scale (0 = no pain; 100 = most intense pain imaginable). Subsequently, a series of 10 heat pulses was delivered at 2-second intervals, and participants rated the painfulness of each stimulus on the same 0–100 scale. This task assessed temporal summation of pain.

#### Conditions and number of trials

Task 1 (Standard QST): 1 condition (32 °C baseline, forearm). CDT: 3 trials; WDT: 3 trials; TSL: 6 trials (3 alternating warm/cold cycles); CPT: 3 trials; HPT: 3 trials.

Task 2 (PHS paradigm): 2 subtasks (Detection, Pain) × 3 baseline temperatures (32 °C, 38 °C, 44 °C). TSL Detection: 6 trials per baseline temperature (plus 3 practice trials at the first baseline). TSL Pain: 6 trials per baseline temperature.

Task 3 (Temporal Summation): 1 single heat pulse followed by 1 series of 10 heat pulses at 2-second intervals.

#### Behavioural Measures

Task 1: Thermal thresholds in °C for CDT, WDT, TSL, CPT, and HPT; presence of paradoxical heat sensation (PHS) during TSL trials (binary: yes/no).

Task 2: Thermal detection and pain thresholds in °C at each temperature condition (32 °C, 38 °C, 44 °C); perceived quality of temperature change (warm, cold, hot, painfully hot, painfully cold); presence of PHS (binary).

Task 3: Pain ratings on a 0–100 numerical rating scale for each of the 10 heat pulses (plus the initial single-pulse rating).

#### Related publications

Mitchell, A. G., Fischer Ehmsen, J., Basińska, M., Courtin, A. S., Böhme, R. A., Sardeto Deolindo, C., Allen, M. G., Sandberg, K., & Fardo, F. (2024). Thermal contrast enhancement predicts paradoxical heat sensation. Communications Psychology, 2(1), 37. https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-024-00083-8

The PHS paradigm (Task 2) is based on the experimental procedure described in this paper, which used a modified TSL protocol with elevated baseline temperatures to investigate the role of thermal contrast enhancement in paradoxical heat sensation.
