#### Detailed description

The Musical Ear Test (MET) is a short, objective test of musical competence (the ability to perceive, remember, and discriminate music) that works for both trained musicians and people with no musical background, with practically no ceiling or floor effects. It consists of 104 trials on which participants judge whether two short musical phrases, played one after the other, are identical or not. It targets two fundamental dimensions of music — melody (pitch and contour) and rhythm — which are tested in two separate subtests of 52 trials each.

**Melody subtest.** 52 pairs of melodic phrases are played with a sampled grand piano. Each melody contains 3–8 tones, lasts one measure, and is played at 100 bpm. On "different" trials the second phrase contains a single pitch violation. For half of these (13), the pitch change also alters the melodic contour. 25 trials contain non-diatonic tones. Of the remaining 27, 20 are in a major and 7 in a minor key.

**Rhythm subtest.** 52 trials are played on a single wood block accompanied by a lower-amplitude metronome. Each sequence contains 4–11 beats, lasts one measure, and is played at 100 bpm. On "different" trials the second phrase contains a single rhythmic change. Rhythmic complexity is varied by including triplets in 21 trials. The remaining 31 trials contain only even subdivisions, and 37 trials begin on the downbeat.

In each subtest, half of the trials (26) are "same" and half (26) are "different", presented in randomized order. Before each subtest, participants receive two example trials with feedback (no feedback is given during the test itself). Participants respond on each trial by crossing one of two boxes ("same" / "different") on a response sheet. Stimuli are delivered by laptop through headphones. The full test lasts about 18 minutes. Versions with English and Danish instructions are freely available from the original authors.

**Scoring.** Scoring awards one point for each correct response, with Melody and Rhythm subtest scores each calculated as the percentage of correct answers out of 52. The MET total score can be calculated as the percentage of correct answers out of the sum of these subtests (i.e., 104).

#### Number of participants (raw)

254 (Aarhus)

#### Conditions and number of trials

104 trials in total, in two subtests:

* Melody subtest: 52 trials (26 "same" + 26 "different"), plus 2 example trials with feedback
* Rhythm subtest: 52 trials (26 "same" + 26 "different"), plus 2 example trials with feedback

Within the melody "different" trials, 13 also involve a contour violation; 25 melody trials contain non-diatonic tones. Within the rhythm trials, 21 include triplets and 37 begin on the downbeat. Trial order (and hence difficulty) is randomized within each subtest.

#### Behavioural Measures

* Accuracy — percentage of correct same/different judgments, scored for the melody subtest, the rhythm subtest, and the total test
* Internal consistency — Cronbach's α = 0.85 (total), 0.82 (melody), 0.69 (rhythm) (from the Corrigendum)
